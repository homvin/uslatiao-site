What Is Latiao (辣条)?

The chewy chili strip that became a Chinese childhood staple — and why it's showing up in snack boxes everywhere.

Short answer: latiao (辣条) is a chewy, springy strip made from wheat gluten, seasoned and tossed in chili oil with Sichuan pepper. It tastes savory first, then numbing-hot — the signature mala tingle on the lips rather than a straight burn. We stock it in the US and ship within 1 business day.

Texture and taste

Latiao (辣条) is a snack made from wheat gluten that's been seasoned, steamed, and tossed in chili oil with Sichuan pepper. The texture is the surprise: springy and chewy, closer to a thin noodle than a cracker. It's savory first, then the heat builds, and a few seconds later the Sichuan pepper makes your lips tingle.

What's actually in it?

The base is wheat flour and water formed into strips. After steaming, it's dressed with soybean or chili oil, ground chili, Sichuan pepper, salt, sugar, and aromatics like garlic. Good latiao lists every ingredient on the pack — and on our product pages, including allergens such as wheat and soy.

Where does it come from?

Latiao grew out of Chinese schoolyards and convenience stores, where it became the default cheap, spicy treat. Over the last decade it crossed into the mainstream and then overseas, riding the same wave as bubble tea and instant noodles. Today it's a recognizable category on its own, not just "that spicy thing."

The mala twist

The defining flavor is mala (麻辣): ma is the numbing tingle from Sichuan pepper, la is the chili heat. People expect pain from spicy food; mala gives a buzzing, almost fizzy numbness instead. That's why "spicy" alone doesn't describe it well — our heat scale accounts for both the burn and the tingle.

How do you eat it?

Straight from the pack, no plate required. Each strip is pre-cut into bite-sized pieces. Keep a cold drink nearby if you're new to it — milk or a sweet drink takes the edge off better than water. It pairs well with plain rice crackers or a cold beverage, and it's the kind of snack that disappears faster than you expect.

Frequently asked

Is latiao vegan?

Most latiao is vegan — wheat gluten, oil, chili and spices with no meat or dairy. Always check the ingredients line on the product page, since recipes vary by maker.

What does "mala" mean?

Mala (麻辣) is the Sichuan pairing of ma (numbing, from Sichuan pepper) and la (spicy, from chili). The numbness is a tingle, not pain.
