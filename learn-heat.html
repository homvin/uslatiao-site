Chinese Heat Levels, Explained

Our Heat 1–5 scale, the numbing magic of Sichuan pepper, and how to find your own spice ceiling.

In short: every snack is rated on our Heat 1–5 scale — 1–2 is a mild warm hum, 3 is a comfortable tingle, 4 is bold with lingering mala numbness, and 5 is extra hot for regular spicy-food eaters. Mala (麻辣) is Sichuan pepper: a buzzing numbness on the lips, not pain. If hot Cheetos are your ceiling, start at Heat 2–3.

Why a 1–5 scale?

"Spicy" means different things to different people. A single number hides too much, so we rate every snack on a 1-to-5 scale that captures both the burn from chili and the tingle from Sichuan pepper. Heat 1 is gentle; Heat 5 is a commitment.

The levels

Heat 1–2 (Mild): a warm hum, more flavor than fire. Good for kids and cautious first-timers.
Heat 3 (Medium): a real tingle and a clear chili presence, but comfortable for most people.
Heat 4 (Hot): bold and savory with a lingering numbness — our Mala Strips Original lives here.
Heat 5 (Extra Hot): for regular spicy-food eaters who finished Heat 4 and wanted more.

What "mala" adds

Western heat is mostly capsaicin burn. Sichuan mala (麻辣) layers chili (la) with Sichuan pepper (ma), which doesn't burn — it buzzes. Your lips and tongue feel fizzy and slightly numb for a few minutes. It's a sensation, not pain, and it's the reason people describe our strips as "numbing, not just hot."

Finding your ceiling

Don't guess — taste. The First-Timer Variety Pack puts Heat 1 through 5 in one box, so you can climb the scale in small steps. Your ceiling is the highest heat you still enjoy, not the highest you can survive. Most people land between 3 and 4.

Cooling down

If a level surprises you, skip water (it spreads capsaicin). Milk, a sweet drink, or a bite of something plain works fastest. Our chocolate bites are a popular reset between spicy rounds.

Frequently asked

Is Heat 5 the same as ghost-pepper hot?

No. Our scale measures enjoyable eating heat with chili plus Sichuan pepper, not pure capsaicin records. Heat 5 is bold and lingering, not a stunt.

Why does my mouth tingle instead of burning?

That's mala — Sichuan pepper stimulates touch-like receptors, producing a tingle on the lips and tongue. It fades in a few minutes.
